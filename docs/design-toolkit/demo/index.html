What the water uncovers

Keep scrolling and the tide goes out. Each band of the shore is dry for less of the day than the one above it, so the lowest pools hold the strangest residents.

  1. Splash zone

    Above 4.5 m. Dry except in storms.

    Black lichen and small periwinkles that can go weeks without a wave.

  2. Upper shore

    3.5 to 4.5 m. Dry most of the day.

    Channelled wrack, limpets clamped tight, acorn barnacles closed against the sun.

  3. Middle shore

    1.5 to 3.5 m. Exposed twice a day.

    Beadlet anemones, shore crabs under bladderwrack, blennies in the deeper pools.

  4. Lower shore

    Below 1.5 m. Open only near low water.

    Kelp holdfasts, brittlestars, squat lobsters and, on the best spring tides, a cushion star.

Before you go

  • Arrive an hour before low water and turn back as soon as it turns. The lower shore floods first.
  • Put every rock back the way you found it. The underside is someone's roof.
  • Look with your eyes, then a tub. Fill it with pool water, keep it in the shade, and return animals to the same pool.

Tide figures are generated for this demo and are not for navigation.
